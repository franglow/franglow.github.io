03 — Where the web draws the line

What I
can't see.

Browsers block all of this unless you say yes, and they show you an indicator while it's on. That's the web working as it should.

  • Your cameraNeeds your explicit yes. Then a light or icon shows it.
  • Your microphoneSame rule. No silent listening, ever.
  • Your historyThe old :visited trick was closed off in 2010.
  • Your other tabsEach site lives in its own sandbox.
  • Your local networkIn 2025, Meta and Yandex were caught using localhost to link web visits to Android apps. Since Chrome 142, sites must ask first.
  • Your passwordsNever exposed to the page you're on.

04 — The decisions behind it

How this
works.

A page about trust has to earn it. These are the calls I made and why.

01

One file. No framework.

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. GSAP runs the intro timeline; everything else is native browser APIs. It loads fast because there's almost nothing to load.

02

Nothing leaves your device.

No server, no cookies, no analytics. The only outside requests are the fonts (Google) and GSAP (Cloudflare), so those two see your IP, like on most sites.

03

Honest over impressive.

Your city comes from your clock's timezone, not GPS: less precise, nothing sent. When a browser hides something, the page says so instead of guessing.

04

Consent before the creepy part.

The tracker tests run only after you say yes. What they store stays in your browser, and "Forget me" deletes it.

05

Motion with an off switch.

The intro plays once per session, any key skips it, and it never plays if your system asks for reduced motion.

06

Built with AI as the default.

I direct the tools, review what they produce, and own what ships. The concept, the limits and the trade-offs on this page are decisions, not defaults.
